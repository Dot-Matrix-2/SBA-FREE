## Collateral

Lenders must follow the SBA's collateral policy, which generally requires them to take **available business assets and, in many cases, real estate** as collateral for larger loans. For loans over $50,000, lenders typically take what is reasonably available, up to the loan amount.

The SBA does not require a lender to decline a loan solely because the collateral is not enough. A creditworthy business with limited collateral can still be approved, though the lender may ask for more of other strengths.

## Personal guarantees

Anyone who owns **20% or more** of the business must usually provide an **unlimited personal guarantee**. This means that if the business cannot repay, you could be personally responsible. Lenders may also ask for guarantees from other owners or closely related people.

## Owner investment

For startups and changes of ownership, expect the lender to require an **equity injection** from the owner (commonly at least 10% of the project cost).

## Questions to ask a lender

- What collateral will you take, and how will it be valued?
- Who must sign a personal guarantee?
- Is there a lien on my home? (Lenders may take a lien on a residence only in limited situations. Ask.)
