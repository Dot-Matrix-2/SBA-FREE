Eligibility has two parts: the **business** and the **owners**. Each lender also applies its own credit standards on top of the SBA's rules.

## Basic business requirements

- Operates as a **for-profit** business
- Has its principal place of business in the **United States** or its territories
- Qualifies as a **small business** under the SBA's size standards (based on industry, using revenue or number of employees)
- Is in an **eligible industry** (see below)
- Can show a **reasonable owner investment** ("skin in the game")
- Has **tried to find other financing** on reasonable terms and could not, or the loan would not be available without the SBA guarantee

## Owner requirements

- Owners must be of good character, with no unresolved issues such as delinquent federal debt or defaults on prior government-backed loans
- Under current SBA rules, businesses must generally be owned entirely by **U.S. citizens, U.S. nationals, or lawful permanent residents**
- Certain criminal history, recent bankruptcy, or an unpaid federal tax debt can affect eligibility

## Businesses that are generally not eligible

- Businesses primarily engaged in **lending** or financial activities
- **Passive real estate investment** (for example, owning property only to rent to others)
- **Gambling** businesses and others deriving significant revenue from gambling
- **Speculative** businesses (for example, trading or investing)
- **Pyramid** sales plans
- Businesses engaged in activity that is **illegal under federal law**, including marijuana-related businesses
- **Nonprofit**, religious, and government-owned organizations
- Certain businesses of a prurient sexual nature

## Credit and finances

The SBA does not set one universal credit score. In practice, many lenders look for personal credit scores in the high 600s or better, a business that can show repayment ability from cash flow, and a clean history with government debt.
