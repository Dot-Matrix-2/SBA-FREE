If you do not qualify for a standard 7(a) loan, or another program fits better, consider these.

## SBA 504 loans

Long-term, fixed-rate financing for **major fixed assets** such as real estate and heavy equipment. Funded through a partnership of a bank lender and a Certified Development Company (CDC). Often a better fit than 7(a) for large real-estate purchases.

## SBA Microloans

Loans of **up to $50,000** made through nonprofit intermediary lenders. Good for startups, very small businesses, and those with limited credit history. Many intermediaries also provide training.

## SBA Express (a 7(a) variation)

Faster, simpler 7(a) loans of **up to $500,000**, with a lower SBA guarantee. Lenders use their own processes, so approval can be quicker.

## 7(a) Small Loans

7(a) loans of **$350,000 or less** are processed under streamlined rules.

## Disaster loans

Low-interest loans for businesses affected by declared disasters.

## Non-SBA options

Conventional bank loans, credit unions, online lenders, community development financial institutions (CDFIs), and equipment financing. Compare total cost carefully.

For official descriptions, see [sba.gov/funding-programs/loans](https://www.sba.gov/funding-programs/loans).
